Empirically confirmed (real MuJoCo FK, perturbing shoulder_{l,r}_adduct and hip_{l,r}_adduct +/-0.2 rad from neutral and reading hand_{l,r}/foot_{l,r} world Y) that positive adduct = adduction toward the midline, negative = abduction, for both shoulder and hip -- the documented sign convention was correct. The bug was only the range bounds: SHOULDER_ADDUCT_MIN/MAX = -30/+180 deg allowed 180 deg of adduction but only 30 deg of abduction, the mirror of real anatomy (Kapandji 2008: abduction to ~180 deg overhead, adduction capped near 30 deg). Swapped to -180/+30 deg. Hip was checked the same way and is already correct (-45/+30 deg, abduction gets the larger range) -- left untouched, no evidence of a defect. Updated the grip-abduction clamp in base.py to reference the abduction bound explicitly (-SHOULDER_ADDUCT_MIN) instead of abs(SHOULDER_ADDUCT_MIN), which happened to be equivalent but was not self-documenting. Snatch now achieves its documented 0.60 m grip width (was clamped to 0.4585 m) with weld residual ~1.1e-7 m (was ~2.4e-7 m at the narrower width); deadlift/clean_and_jerk/bench_press grip widths were already unaffected by the clamp and are unchanged. The vendored parity standard (_canonical/biomech_parity_standard.json) carries the same mirrored shoulder_adduct limits; added a parity_divergences.json entry citing #438 (never edited the vendored file itself; tracked upstream as Repository_Management#2062). Added a new regression test (test_keyframe_within_joint_ranges.py) asserting every barbell exercise's initial keyframe qpos lies within its own joint's range; this caught a real, separate coupling bug it was not looking for: the wrist-deviate counter-rotation that cancels the shoulder abduction's tilt on the hand (#408/#437) now needs to match the snatch's ~48 deg shoulder abduction, which exceeds the wrist's own +30 deg deviate limit. Clamping the wrist counter-rotation independently reintroduces a ~188 mm grip-weld residual (measured with the real engine), so it is not fixed here -- filed as MuJoCo_Models#440 and carried as a documented, ratcheted exception in the new test.
